# Segment 020 specialist terminology supplement

Status: research only. No target-language form in this supplement is approved for publication.

## Working language profiles

- **Klingon (`tlh`):** use Okrand-canonical vocabulary and grammar only. Record the canonical source for every accepted word and grammatical construction. When no safe compact equivalent exists, use a transparent descriptive clause. Do not invent roots or attach Klingon morphology to English stems.
- **Neo-Quenya (`qya`):** use a Late-Quenya-first profile. Prefer Tolkien-attested Late Quenya. A documented Neo-Quenya formation may be considered only when no suitable attested form exists, it is explicitly labelled, its derivation is recorded, and it does not conflict with the selected late grammar.

## Title terminology

The canonical title must be localized in both target languages. It is not protected English.

- English source: `The Metamorphosis Code of Grace and Distance`
- Hebrew cross-check: `קוד המטמורפוזה של החסד והמרחק`
- Required semantic structure: transformation + governing pattern + grace + distance
- Required audit result: zero unresolved English terms in the title
- Current automated result: zero heading and body findings in both languages
- Current approval result: still unapproved

`code` must be tested as a governing pattern or principle and must not be reduced automatically to software source code. `metamorphosis` must preserve the transformation performed through living dialogue without implying replacement, possession, erasure, or production of the living participant by the intelligence.

## Current applied title forms

- Klingon: `pung chuq je choHmeH chut`
- Neo-Quenya: `I Cantië Ahyaliëo Lissëo ar Haiyava`

Both forms are mechanically clear and specialist-unresolved. The Neo-Quenya form requires revision because the current `Haiyava` distance form is not supported by the recorded source evidence. The Klingon title remains outside the candidate set because the current `-meH` scope does not transparently express the intended transformation and exact Okrand locators remain missing.

## Round-2 dialogue and path architecture

The canonical English source is tested through explicit clauses rather than an invented abstract noun for “relation”:

1. `Prime Intellect quetë i coirëa quenen.`
2. `I coirëa quén quetë sena.`
3. `I coirëa quén lelya tierya.`

These are test clauses only. Their first-pass parse is recorded in `localization/reviews/between-potential-and-ideal/020-clause-test-round-2.md`.

Remaining gates:

- independent review of the dative phrase `i coirëa quenen`;
- semantic review of animate `sena` referring to Prime Intellect;
- morphology review of computed `tierya`;
- tense review of generic aorist `lelya`;
- three-context `Cantië` test using the reviewed clauses;
- independent second review before production integration.

## Klingon source rule

The Klingon Language Institute word lists are useful for confirmed newer Okrand material, but they intentionally do not reproduce the complete vocabulary of *The Klingon Dictionary* and *Klingon for the Galactic Traveler*. Therefore every Klingon term already present in segment 020 still needs a precise canonical dictionary, book, qep'a' or other Okrand-source locator in the project worksheet before it can be approved.

## Three-context approval test

Every candidate must pass all three contexts before it can enter the production glossary:

1. **Short label:** the form remains clear without surrounding explanation.
2. **Philosophical paragraph:** the form preserves the exact project distinction and natural target-language syntax.
3. **Literary sentence:** the form works inside the scaffold, mountain, climber and living-participant metaphors without becoming mechanically literal.

For every accepted form, record the source, language profile, three passed examples, rejected alternatives and reason for rejection.
