# Linguistic review — 430 Market, Credit, Labor, Profit, and Externality

Both drafts preserve the required economic distinctions: the market remains a distributed information system and mirror rather than conscience or god; healthy and sick debt remain distinct according to whether obligation opens or consumes possibility; interest remains a bounded relation among time, expected inflation, risk, liquidity, and policy; labor remains embodied, temporal, and testimonial rather than mere output or cost; profit from creation remains distinct from profit from extraction; externality remains cost excluded from the transaction price. Neither draft is approved.

Klingon blockers: extensive undeclared English remains in the title, headings, formulas, and vocabulary of market, information, price, transaction, supply, demand, credit, debt, interest, inflation, risk premium, liquidity, labor, wage, profit, extraction, externality, private cost, social cost, and financial reporting. Hybrid constructions and English-shaped syntax remain pervasive. A governed Klingon terminology profile is required for market signals, obligation, interest components, embodied labor, recognition, surplus, extraction, bargaining power, and excluded costs.

Neo-Quenya blockers: the title, headings, formulas, and most economic terminology remain in ordinary English. English word order and mixed clauses dominate the treatment of market information, credit, debt, interest, labor, wages, profit, extraction, and externalities. Case marking, compounds, verbal morphology, and the distinctions among signal, conscience, obligation, embodied work, recognized surplus, extraction, and social cost require normalization under one Neo-Quenya profile.

Keep both sources as `draft` with `publication: forbidden`.
