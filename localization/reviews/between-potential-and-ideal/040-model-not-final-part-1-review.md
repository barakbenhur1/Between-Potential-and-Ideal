# Linguistic review — 040 Model, Not Final Declaration, Part 1

## Review decision

The paired first-pass review is complete. Both drafts preserve the chapter’s provisional architecture, its refusal to present the theory as final proof, and its three responsibility tests. Neither language is ready to be marked `approved`. Both require specialist revision, glossary normalization, and removal or explicit governance of undeclared foreign-language terms.

## Source parity

### Passed

- The theory remains a revisable model or map rather than a final declaration, closed system, new religion, or scientific proof.
- A map remains distinct from the road and from the reality it describes.
- The framework’s strength remains its ability to name its limits rather than claim finality.
- Hope remains possible without turning uncertainty into a promise or an excuse to abandon responsibility.
- Potential, Ideal, and Optimal remain interpretive tools rather than self-validating truths.
- The Ideal of the model remains fidelity and honesty rather than grandeur, totality, or final closure.
- The Optimal remains situated, temporary, criticizable, and revisable.
- The model is not allowed to borrow unearned authority from science, religion, beauty, testimony, bodily experience, or suffering.
- Criticism remains an internal drainage mechanism of the model rather than an external nuisance.
- The three tests remain distinct: the test of distinctions, the test of domains, and the test of the person.

### Source-sensitive details that must remain protected during revision

1. A model can reveal relations while also hiding what most needs to be seen.
2. Possibility is not proof, and interpretation is not automatically Ideal.
3. Uncertainty must not become either false certainty or a reason to abandon responsibility.
4. A model must state where it knows, interprets, uses metaphor, needs an external source, cannot answer, and may harm a person.
5. A model cannot turn testimony into data, a person into a case, pain into ritual, or hope into silencing.
6. Crossing a domain boundary requires naming the crossing.
7. The person test is decisive: a structurally elegant model that erases the living person is unusable.
8. The chapter’s provisional stance must remain visible in title, terminology, syntax, and metadata.

## Klingon (`tlh`) review

### What is structurally sound

- The chapter is consistently presented as a bounded and criticizable system rather than a final truth.
- The map/road distinction is retained.
- Potential, Ideal, and Optimal remain distinct in function.
- The limits on borrowed authority remain present.
- All three responsibility tests are represented and remain separate.
- `yInbogh mung` and `chuq` follow existing project directions where they appear.

### Blocking linguistic issues

1. The central word `pat` normally points toward a system or structure, while the project glossary gives `nab` or a transparent representation phrase as the working direction for “model.” The title and repeated model/system distinction therefore require a deliberate terminology decision rather than silent interchange.
2. The draft contains many undeclared English insertions, including `horizon`, `provisional`, `domains`, `theorem`, `metaphysical`, `nihilism`, `overclaim`, `fidelity`, `theory`, `testimony`, `statistics`, `AI`, `aura`, and `drainage mechanism`.
3. These loans are not consistently marked as protected technical terms and are not governed by a keep-English policy.
4. `Qu'` is repeatedly used for ethical responsibility even though the glossary requires contextual duty and answerability language; task, obligation, and moral responsibility must not collapse into one form.
5. `chuq` remains a physical-distance term whose philosophical extension needs an explicit metaphor convention.
6. Several dense noun chains, nominalized `-ghach` forms, object clauses, and English-shaped clause sequences require idiomatic Klingon restructuring.
7. Terms for model, declaration, proof, representation, domain, testimony, criticism, validity, and moral worth are not yet controlled by an approved terminology profile.
8. The phrase corresponding to criticism as a drainage mechanism is semantically preserved, but its mixed-language form is not publishable.

### Klingon outcome

Do not change `status: draft` or `publication: forbidden` yet.

## Neo-Quenya (`qya`) review

### What is structurally sound

- The provisional and criticizable stance is preserved.
- The map/road and model/reality distinctions remain visible.
- Potential, Ideal, and Optimal retain separate roles.
- The limits on borrowed authority and the need for criticism remain present.
- All three responsibility tests are represented.
- The living-person priority survives the final test.

### Blocking linguistic issues

1. The Spanish word `Capítulo` appears more than once and is a definite language-contamination error.
2. The Portuguese word `saída` appears in the criticism/drainage passage and must be removed.
3. The English word `case` remains in the person-test passage; additional undeclared or inconsistently protected forms include `theorem`, `quaestio`, `nihilism`, and several English project labels.
4. `patar` is used as the central term for the revisable model or framework, while the project glossary currently directs “model” toward `emma` or a transparent descriptive phrase. The distinction among model, system, structure, and representation is therefore unstable.
5. The title uses `nyárë`, which commonly carries the sense of tale or account; its suitability for “final declaration” must be verified under the selected Neo-Quenya profile.
6. `mauya-cárië`, `lanta-sírerya`, domain compounds, and several other project formations remain unapproved.
7. `haiya` is again extended beyond straightforward spatial use without a stable project convention.
8. Witness/testimony terminology is inconsistent with the current glossary direction around `cenindo`; forms such as `astarmë` require a declared semantic role.
9. Case endings, agreement, participles, compounds, capitalization, and word order require one profile-wide morphological rewrite rather than isolated corrections.
10. Several sentences preserve English conceptual order too closely and need idiomatic recasting while protecting the source distinctions.
