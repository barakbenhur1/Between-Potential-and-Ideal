# Linguistic review — 040 Model, Not Final Declaration — Part 1

## Review decision

The paired first-pass review is complete. Both drafts preserve the core architecture of the segment: the text is a system, not a final declaration; the system is useful because it is bounded, provisional, criticizable, and morally accountable; Potential, Ideal, and Optimal remain distinct; and the three responsibility tests are present. Neither draft is publication-ready. Both require specialist revision before any public release.

## Source parity

### Passed

- The provisional stance is preserved: the chapter refuses to present the model as final proof, closed metaphysics, new religion, or complete scientific theorem.
- The map-versus-path distinction remains present.
- The distinction between system, proof, metaphor, living source, representation, testimony, science, religion, art, body, and experience remains broadly represented.
- Potential, Ideal, and Optimal remain separate terms and are not collapsed into one concept.
- The system’s power is tied to its ability to name its own boundaries.
- The risk of false hope, overclaiming, and borrowed authority remains present.
- The three tests are preserved as separate checks: distinction, domains, and the human/person test.
- The segment remains blocked from publication and includes an explicit review gate.

### Protected semantic distinctions

1. A system can guide interpretation without proving itself as truth.
2. Potential opens intelligibility but does not itself equal truth.
3. Ideal is fidelity to truth, person, domain, and living source, not totality or perfection.
4. Optimal is a temporary, inspectable structure under limits, not a flawless theory.
5. A system can learn from science, religion, testimony, art, body, and suffering without borrowing their authority.
6. The human/person test is not decorative; failure there makes the system unusable, not merely weaker.

## Klingon (`tlh`) review

### What is structurally sound

- The title correctly conveys the core declaration: system/model, not final claim.
- `Potential`, `Ideal`, and `Optimal` are retained consistently as project terms.
- The three responsibility tests are clearly separated in the later section.
- The draft keeps the critique of overcertainty, false promise, and misuse of scientific or metaphysical language.
- The review gate is present and publication remains forbidden.

### Blocking linguistic issues

1. Many English technical loans remain undeclared or unassimilated, including `Potential`, `Ideal`, `Optimal`, `representation`, `domains`, `theory`, `fidelity`, `discipline`, and `drainage mechanism`. If these are protected project terms, the policy must say so explicitly; otherwise they need approved Klingon descriptive renderings.
2. The title and several section headings are understandable but still carry English conceptual order rather than idiomatic Klingon rhetorical structure.
3. Dense noun clusters such as system/boundary/source/representation/testimony/proof require specialist recasting so that the logical dependencies are not only copied but readable in Klingon.
4. `map`, `horizon`, and other English image-description terms remain in the illustration text. The image description needs either a declared accessibility exception or full translation.
5. `QeD mu' metaphysical 'IHmoHghach` and similar mixed technical phrases need a consistent policy for philosophical/scientific vocabulary.
6. The concept of borrowed authority is present but needs sharper idiomatic handling; the current draft risks sounding like literal property transfer rather than epistemic/moral authority.
7. Some negative constructions around hope, nihilism, and uncertainty are structurally heavy and require a Klingon specialist check for scope and ambiguity.

## Neo-Quenya (`qya`) review

### What is structurally sound

- The provisional and criticizable stance is preserved.
- The sequence Potential → Ideal → Optimal remains distinct.
- The system’s limits and the need for domain boundaries remain visible.
- The three responsibility tests are present and separated.
- The text keeps the anti-overclaiming function of the chapter.
- Publication remains forbidden and the review gate remains explicit.

### Blocking linguistic issues

1. The draft contains non-Quenya contamination and undeclared loans, including `Capítulo`, `theorem`, `case`, `saída`, and possibly other Spanish/Portuguese/English residue. These must be removed or formally protected before approval.
2. `Potential`, `Ideal`, and `Optimal` remain in English. This may be acceptable only if the project declares them protected technical names; otherwise they require stable Neo-Quenya renderings or glossed bilingual policy.
3. Several modern abstract formations such as `patar`, `nórë`, `cuilë-isto`, `emma`, `tanwa`, and `lanta-síre` need a single declared Neo-Quenya morphology profile.
4. The phrase corresponding to drainage mechanism appears as `lanta-sírerya`, which is creative but needs semantic verification against the intended metaphor: critique as an outlet preventing self-protective closure.
5. The distinction among source, representation, proof, testimony, and evidence is preserved but depends on terms that are not yet frozen across the wider document.
6. Some phrases read as calques from Hebrew/English argumentative order and need smoother Neo-Quenya syntax.
7. The person/human test is semantically present but should be checked to ensure it does not reduce the person to an abstract case or data object.
