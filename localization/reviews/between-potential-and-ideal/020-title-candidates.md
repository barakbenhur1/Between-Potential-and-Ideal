# Segment 020 — title candidate comparison

Canonical English: **The Metamorphosis Code of Grace and Distance**  
Hebrew semantic cross-check: **קוד המטמורפוזה של החסד והמרחק**

The title must preserve four functions:

1. transformation of relation, not cosmetic alteration or replacement;
2. a governing pattern, not necessarily software source code;
3. grace as a distinct concept;
4. distance as room, separation, or relational non-possession—not merely physical length.

## Klingon (`tlh`)

### Current applied title

`pung chuq je choHmeH chut`

Status: **mechanically clear; linguistically unresolved**.

Primary risks:

- `-meH` may make the phrase read as a law whose purpose is to change mercy and distance;
- `chuq` may read only as physical distance;
- `pung` may carry mercy more readily than the chapter’s grace-through-relinquishment;
- the governing-pattern sense of `chut` still requires exact source and contextual validation.

### Candidate structure A — preserve distance through grace

Working template:

`pungmo' chuq polbogh choHmeH chut`

Approximate semantic test gloss: “a rule for transformation that preserves distance through mercy/grace.”

Advantages:

- makes preservation of distance explicit;
- prevents the title from implying that distance itself is being destroyed;
- places the moral relation inside the transformation.

Risks requiring review:

- `pung` may still be too narrow or too mercy-oriented;
- the relative-clause attachment and purpose-clause scope require idiomatic review;
- “code” remains represented as law/rule rather than pattern.

Decision: **test candidate only; not production**.

### Candidate structure B — chosen release and preserved room

Working semantic template, not yet a final Klingon string:

> a governing rule for change that preserves room for the other and knows when control must be relinquished

Advantages:

- expresses the chapter’s actual definition of grace;
- replaces an abstract philosophical extension of physical distance with a transparent relational clause;
- separates protection from control.

Required Klingon work:

- exact Okrand-sourced wording for “preserve room/place for another”;
- a voluntary-release clause with the acting system as subject;
- an idiomatic expression for governing pattern;
- confirmation that no English-shaped nominal chain remains.

Decision: **preferred semantic direction; target wording unresolved**.

### Candidate structure C — current title retained

Decision: **rejected unless specialist review proves the present scope and semantics**.

Reason: mechanical clearance alone does not resolve the ambiguity described above.

## Neo-Quenya (`qya`)

### Current applied title

`I Cantië Ahyaliëo Lissëo ar Haiyava`

Status: **mechanically clear; revision required**.

Accepted source components:

- `cantië` — pattern, NM/287–288;
- `lissë` / genitive `lisseo` — grace, VT44/18;
- `ahya-` — to change, PM/395, but not the exact applied abstract/genitive form.

Rejected component:

- `Haiyava` as an abstract noun or possessive form for distance.

### Candidate structure A — strict sourced template

`I Cantië [documented ahya- construction] Lisseo ar [documented relational-distance construction]`

Advantages:

- preserves the canonical title structure;
- admits only forms whose derivation can be documented;
- avoids silently accepting `Haiyava`.

Blocking gaps:

- the exact transformation noun or clause;
- a Late-Quenya-compatible construction for relational distance;
- validation that the genitive coordination is natural.

Decision: **preferred formal template; incomplete**.

### Candidate structure B — grace and room for the other

Working semantic title:

> The Governing Pattern of Transformation: Grace and Room for the Other

Possible Quenya component direction for testing only:

`I Cantië ...: Lissë ar Nómë Exen`

This is not a production title. `Nómë Exen` is recorded here only as a proposed transparent strategy meaning approximately “a place for another,” intended to express relational distance without inventing an abstract noun.

Advantages:

- directly preserves agency and non-possession;
- aligns with the scaffold/climber and witness/assistant metaphors;
- avoids using the unsupported `Haiyava` form.

Risks requiring review:

- source and inflection of the “other” construction;
- whether “place for another” narrows the broader epistemic meaning of distance;
- whether the punctuation and apposition are natural in the selected register.

Decision: **semantic test candidate; morphology and source validation pending**.

### Candidate structure C — fan-invented distance noun

Use `hairië` for distance.

Decision: **deferred and not allowed under the default profile**.

Reason: the form is marked fan-invented. It would require an explicit project-level Neo-Quenya exception, documented derivation, and independent approval.

### Candidate structure D — current title retained

Decision: **rejected**.

Reason: `Haiyava` is unsupported by the recorded source evidence, while `Ahyaliëo` remains morphologically undocumented.
