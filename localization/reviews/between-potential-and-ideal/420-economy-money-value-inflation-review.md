# Linguistic review — 420 Economy, Money, Value, and Inflation

Both drafts preserve the required economic boundaries: value remains relational without being reduced to imagination; money remains a medium or sign of recognized potential rather than value itself; commodity, representative, and fiat money remain distinct; purchasing-power and real-exchange-rate formulas remain directionally and contextually bounded; the quantity equation remains an identity rather than a simple causal theory of inflation. Neither draft is approved.

Klingon blockers: extensive undeclared English remains in the title, headings, formulas, examples, and vocabulary of economy, money, value, price, interest, wage, debt, inflation, growth, exchange rate, trust, credit, contract, ownership, fiat, gold, purchasing power, price level, central bank, policy, and shocks. Entire list items and explanatory phrases remain in English. Hybrid constructions and English-shaped syntax are pervasive. A single governed Klingon profile is required for value relations, monetary forms, trust, price measures, exchange-rate direction, inflation, and the interpretation of every formula.

Neo-Quenya blockers: the title, headings, formulas, examples, lists, and most economic terminology remain in ordinary English. English word order and mixed clauses dominate the treatment of value, money, gold, fiat systems, purchasing power, exchange rates, and inflation. Case marking, compounds, verbal morphology, mathematical explanation, and the distinction among nominal, real, relational, and causal claims require normalization under one Neo-Quenya profile.

Keep both sources as `draft` with `publication: forbidden`.
