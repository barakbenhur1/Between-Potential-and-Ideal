# Linguistic review — 470 Civil Society, Public Language, Policy, and Repair

Both drafts preserve the required distinctions: civil society remains shared life outside direct state command, neither swallowed nor abandoned by the state; public language remains a medium for conflict before conflict becomes force; majority and minority remain reciprocal tests inside a shared world; policy remains distinct from declaration and procedure from purposeless machinery; measurement remains both visibility and concealment; the segment ends with bounded power, appeal, non-dehumanizing conflict, and repair rather than redemption. Neither draft is approved.

Klingon blockers: extensive undeclared English remains in the title, headings, formulas, and vocabulary of civil society, public language, policy, implementation, measurement, majority, minority, accountability, participation, institutional decay, appeal, coercion, control, and repair. Hybrid constructions and English-shaped syntax remain pervasive. One governed Klingon profile is required for voluntary association, public discourse, reciprocal political tests, policy execution, measurement limits, bounded authority, appeal, and non-dehumanizing conflict.

Neo-Quenya blockers: the title, headings, formulas, and most civic and policy terminology remain in ordinary English. English word order and mixed clauses dominate the treatment of civil society, public language, majority and minority, policy, procedure, measurement, institutional decay, coercion, and repair. Case marking, compounds, verbal morphology, and the distinctions among state command, association, rhetoric, implementation, visibility, concealment, appeal, and bounded power require normalization under one Neo-Quenya profile.
