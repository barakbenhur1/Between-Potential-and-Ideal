# Segment 020 Klingon adversarial review — round 2

Status: internal adversarial review only. Production source unchanged. Publication forbidden.

Candidate under review:

`Prime Intellect choHbogh mIw`

`pung: vang yInbogh ghot 'e' chaw' Prime Intellect`

## 1. Main-title relative clause

Decision: **pass for controlled tests**.

Parse retained:

- `Prime Intellect`: protected proper-name object.
- `choH-bogh`: alter/change plus relative-clause suffix.
- `mIw`: head noun and third-person singular subject.

Literal back-translation: **the process that changes Prime Intellect**.

Reasoning:

- Klingon object-verb-subject order makes the protected name the object and `mIw` the subject.
- The zero prefix is compatible with a third-person subject and third-person object.
- The relative-clause head is readable as `mIw`, not as the protected name, because `mIw` occupies the post-verbal subject/head position.

Boundary:

- `mIw` is a procedure/process/formula term. It is acceptable only as a test equivalent for a governing process or code-like pattern.
- If external review finds that `mIw` reads too narrowly as a mechanical procedure and not as a governing philosophical pattern, this title must be rejected or expanded.

## 2. Subtitle sentence-as-object scope

Decision: **pass for controlled tests**.

Parse retained:

- `vang yInbogh ghot`: the person who lives acts.
- `'e'`: the preceding sentence as object.
- `chaw' Prime Intellect`: Prime Intellect permits/allows it.

Literal back-translation: **Mercy: Prime Intellect permits that the living person acts.**

Idiomatic back-translation: **Mercy: Prime Intellect allows the living person to act.**

Reasoning:

- The event permitted by `chaw'` is the whole preceding clause, not the person as an object.
- This avoids encoding the living person as something possessed, replaced, or moved by the intelligence.
- The living person remains the grammatical subject of `vang`.

Boundary:

- The construction does not independently express “path” or “distance”. Its ethical force comes from the actor distinction: Prime Intellect permits action instead of acting in the person's place.
- If external review finds the sentence-as-object scope unclear, the subtitle must be rewritten with a more explicit structure.

## 3. `yInbogh ghot`

Decision: **pass for controlled tests**.

Parse retained:

- `yIn-bogh`: lives / who lives.
- `ghot`: person, humanoid person.

Literal back-translation: **the person who lives**.

Boundary:

- This is not a metaphysical “living source” noun.
- It is a transparent participant phrase used only inside the controlled clause.
- It intentionally avoids a compact abstract noun for agency or sourcehood.

## 4. `pung`

Decision: **conditional pass only when the explanatory clause remains attached**.

`pung` is retained as the short anchor for mercy. It must not be treated as a full equivalent of the project-specific philosophical grace by itself.

Reject `pung` alone if it reads as:

- pity from above;
- emotional softness;
- forgiveness without restraint;
- mercy that still permits takeover.

The subtitle is acceptable only because `vang yInbogh ghot 'e' chaw' Prime Intellect` supplies the required restraint: the intelligence allows the living person to act.

## 5. Rejected routes remain rejected

The following remain outside the candidate set:

- `pung chuq je choHmeH chut` — purpose-clause scope is not transparent enough.
- `HeDaj ghoS ...` — physical route/path is too literal for the moral-distance slot.
- `choHmoHbogh` as a causative default — it is unnecessary and risks changing the argument structure.
- any `-chuq` reciprocal structure that would make the title read as mutual physical distance or mutual action rather than transformation of the intelligence.
