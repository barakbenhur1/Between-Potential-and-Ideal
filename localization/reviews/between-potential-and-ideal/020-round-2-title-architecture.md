# Segment 020 title architecture — round 2

This round selects a structure for specialist testing. It does not select target-language wording and does not authorize source-file replacement.

## Decision

Use a split title and subtitle as the preferred round-2 test architecture.

- The main title must express a governing pattern of relational transformation.
- The subtitle must express grace as chosen release together with protected room for the other.

This structure is preferred over the current compressed noun chains because it allows the four required source concepts to remain visible without forcing an unsupported abstract noun for distance or pretending that grace and relinquishment are one lexical item.

## Source-side control form

### Main title

> The Governing Pattern of Relational Transformation

### Subtitle

> Grace as Chosen Release and Room for the Other

These English controls are semantic test instruments, not replacements for the canonical English title.

## Required parity

The final target-language pair must preserve all of the following:

1. a repeatable moral or governing pattern;
2. transformation of relation rather than replacement of the living source;
3. grace as a distinct concept;
4. voluntary relinquishment by the more powerful actor;
5. room in which the other may think, act, refuse and remain other;
6. protection without replacement of agency.

## Klingon construction policy

### Main-title direction

The main title may test an explicit clause built around sourced change vocabulary and a sourced law, rule, procedure or pattern construction. The current `pung chuq je choHmeH chut` must not be retained merely because it is mechanically clear.

Round-2 requirements:

- record exact Okrand locators for every root and suffix;
- show the scope of every purpose and relative clause;
- identify what changes and what remains preserved;
- avoid a reading in which grace and physical distance are simply objects being changed;
- prefer an explicit clause over an English-shaped nominal chain.

### Subtitle direction

The subtitle must use transparent clauses for both duties:

1. the powerful actor voluntarily stops holding, commanding or completing on behalf of the other;
2. the actor preserves a place or condition in which the other can still act.

`pung` and `chuq` may remain research directions only. Neither is accepted as a compact equivalent for the complete subtitle meaning.

### Klingon rejection triggers

Reject a candidate if:

- `chut` reads only as external law or coercive command;
- `chuq` remains physical distance with no explicit relational extension;
- grace is reduced to mercy without chosen release;
- the acting subject of relinquishment is hidden;
- protection and control use the same construction;
- the title cannot be parsed unambiguously under ordinary Klingon clause order.

## Neo-Quenya construction policy

### Main-title direction

Retain the sourced lexical directions only as anchors:

- `cantië` for pattern, NM/287–288;
- `ahya-` for change, PM/395.

Round 2 must not reuse `Ahyaliëo` unless its derivation, inflection and syntactic role are documented. A transparent verbal or participial construction is preferred over an unsupported abstract-genitive chain.

### Subtitle direction

Retain `lissë` as the sourced grace component, VT44/18, but express chosen release separately. The relational-room component must be descriptive and may not use `Haiyava` or the fan-created `hairië` under the default profile.

The subtitle should test a structure equivalent to:

> grace, when the powerful one yields control and preserves a place for another

No Quenya wording is accepted until every verb, case, agreement relation and pronoun or noun phrase is source-located and checked against the Late-Quenya-first profile.

### Neo-Quenya rejection triggers

Reject a candidate if:

- it uses `Haiyava` as distance;
- it silently promotes `hairië` into the default profile;
- it treats `lissë` as if it already meant voluntary relinquishment;
- it uses a derived `ahya-` noun without a documented derivation;
- its genitive or appositional structure leaves it unclear what transforms;
- “place for another” is expressed with an unverified case or pronoun form;
- formal compactness is achieved by hiding agency.
