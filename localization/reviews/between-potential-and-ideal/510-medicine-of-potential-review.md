# Linguistic review — 510 Medicine of Potential

Both drafts preserve the required medical safeguards: the body remains a living reality rather than merely an idea, metaphor, signal set, or case; the chapter remains inside scientific medicine rather than opposing science with intuition; categories remain necessary handles for action without becoming the whole person; the responsible-health relation remains a boundary map rather than a medical formula; both failures remain visible—cancelling the person in the name of measurement and cancelling measurement in the name of the person; AI remains a supervised scaffold that returns responsibility to clinicians and patients; the explicit no-medical-advice disclaimer remains present. Neither draft is approved.

Klingon blockers: extensive undeclared English remains in the title, headings, relation map, disclaimer, and vocabulary of body, pain, diagnosis, treatment, patient, scan, protocol, risk, evidence, consent, continuity, commercialization, automation, AI, clinician, rehabilitation, quality of life, prevention, and systemic change. Hybrid constructions and English-shaped syntax remain pervasive. A governed Klingon profile is required for clinical categories, diagnostic horizons, supervised AI, consent, continuity, responsibility, measurement, care, and disclaimer language.

Neo-Quenya blockers: the title, headings, relation map, disclaimer, and nearly all medical terminology remain in ordinary English. English word order and mixed clauses dominate the treatment of body, diagnosis, treatment, patient, protocol, evidence, consent, continuity, AI, clinician responsibility, rehabilitation, quality of life, prevention, and care. Case marking, compounds, verbal morphology, and the distinctions among symptom, finding, diagnosis, horizon, measurement, judgment, supervised assistance, and medical advice require normalization under one Neo-Quenya profile.

Keep both sources as `draft` with `publication: forbidden`.
