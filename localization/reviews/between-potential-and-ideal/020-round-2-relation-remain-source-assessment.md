# Segment 020 relation/remain source assessment — round 2

This assessment records the focused search required by the `Cantië` philosophical frame. It approves no sentence or title.

## Required frame

> The governing pattern changes the relation without replacing the living source.

Already retained:

- `cantië` — pattern, NM/287–288;
- `ahya-` — to change, PM/395.

Still required:

1. a sourced interpersonal relation construction;
2. a Late-Quenya-compatible predicate for remaining or being preserved.

## `har-`

The pinned Eldamo teaching glossary records `har-` for both “sit” and “stay”. Its displayed past form for this direction carries a reformulation marker.

Decision: **research candidate only**.

It does not yet pass because:

- the exact primary-source locator for the “stay” sense is not recorded here;
- staying in a place is not automatically remaining oneself or remaining in existence;
- tense, agreement and negative syntax remain unreviewed.

## `lemya-`

The project drafts contain unapproved uses of `lemya-`, but the focused search did not establish a Late-Quenya-first source decision for the required sense.

Decision: **not admitted**.

Draft usage and fan-composition usage cannot independently validate the verb. “Remain”, “continue”, “dwell” and “endure” must not be merged without source evidence.

## `ser-`

The teaching glossary records `ser-` for rest and tarry.

Decision: **rejected for the current frame**.

Resting or tarrying does not safely state that a living person remains while a relation changes.

## Possession wording

A construction based on having, holding or possession is rejected. The chapter requires preservation without possession and must not encode the living source as an object held by the system.

## Relation result

No source-approved Late-Quenya construction for the required interpersonal relation has been selected. The draft `ovantë` remains unsupported and is not admitted.

Still-open strategies:

- a sourced relation or bond noun with documented interpersonal scope;
- a reciprocal construction naming both persons;
- two transparent clauses that avoid an abstract relation noun;
- a title restructuring that preserves relational transformation without inventing a compact noun.
