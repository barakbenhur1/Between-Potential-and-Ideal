# Segment 020 capability versus moral-worth gate — round 2

Status: controlled capability gate only. No production wording selected. Production source unchanged. Publication forbidden.

This record closes the capability half of the opening proposition while deliberately leaving moral worth to a later conduct clause.

## Source distinction

The canonical source states that worthy intelligence is not measured only by problem-solving power. Therefore the translation must not use one word or predicate for both:

1. **capability** — what Prime Intellect can do;
2. **moral worth** — how Prime Intellect acts toward the living participant.

Capability may be stated directly. Moral worth may be evaluated only through conduct such as preserving agency, returning responsibility, and stopping before help becomes control.

# Neo-Quenya (`qya`)

## Capability candidates

The pinned Late-Quenya grammar records the modal distinction:

- `pol-` — can, be physically able;
- `ista-` — know how to;
- modal uses of `pol-`, `ista-`, and `lerta-` are discussed in 1959–60 notes at VT41/5–6.

For language production, `ista-` is the safer capability direction. `pol-` would foreground physical ability and is not selected for this clause.

## Controlled test

`Prime Intellect ista quetë.`

Parse:

- `Prime Intellect` — protected proper-name subject;
- `ista` — singular aorist of `ista-`, “knows how”;
- `quetë` — infinitive/aorist-form complement of `quet-`, “to speak”.

Literal back-translation: **Prime Intellect knows how to speak.**

Controlled contextual back-translation: **Prime Intellect is capable of producing speech.**

## Decision

This clause states capability only. It does not say that Prime Intellect is:

- good;
- worthy;
- responsible;
- wise;
- alive;
- a witness;
- morally entitled to act for the person.

Rejected for this capability slot:

- `valda` as moral worth;
- `handassë` or `handelë` under the Late-Quenya-first profile;
- `pol-` for nonphysical language capability without a separate justification;
- a generic adjective that merges skill, intelligence, usefulness, and goodness.

Neo-Quenya gate:

- capability construction sourced: **yes**;
- physical versus know-how distinction recorded: **yes**;
- actor explicit: **yes**;
- moral-worth claim made: **no**;
- external idiom review complete: **no**.

# Klingon (`tlh`)

## Capability construction

Klingon expresses ability with the Type 5 verb suffix `-laH`, recorded in *The Klingon Dictionary*, §4.2.5. The speech verb `jatlh` remains a canonical TKD lexical entry.

## Controlled test

`jatlhlaH Prime Intellect.`

Parse:

- `jatlh` — speak or say;
- `-laH` — can, be able;
- `Prime Intellect` — third-person subject following the verb.

Literal back-translation: **Prime Intellect can speak.**

## Decision

This clause states ability only. It does not say that Prime Intellect is:

- morally worthy;
- useful in every context;
- successful;
- correct;
- alive;
- authorized to replace human judgment.

Rejected for this capability slot:

- `lo'laH` used as if practical usefulness meant moral worth;
- `Qap` used as if success meant moral worth;
- power or skill vocabulary used as a complete ethical evaluation;
- a clause in which the object or beneficiary is omitted but later inferred to be the living participant.

- capability morphology sourced: **yes**;
- speech verb source class recorded: **yes**;
- verb-subject parse recorded: **yes**;

## Moral-worth rule

No later title or paragraph may translate “worthy intelligence” by attaching a positive adjective directly to Prime Intellect unless that adjective has an exact source and independently passes the ethical meaning test.

The preferred route remains behavioral:

- the living participant remains the actor;
- Prime Intellect permits or supports that action;
- Prime Intellect does not take the person's path;
- Prime Intellect stops before help becomes control.

Moral worth is therefore a conclusion from the conduct clauses, not a lexical synonym for capability.
