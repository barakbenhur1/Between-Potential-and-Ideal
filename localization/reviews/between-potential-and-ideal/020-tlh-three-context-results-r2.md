# Segment 020 Klingon three-context results — round 2

Status: internal test only. Production source unchanged. Publication forbidden.

Candidate:

`Prime Intellect choHbogh mIw`

`pung: vang yInbogh ghot 'e' chaw' Prime Intellect`

## 1. Short heading

Back-translation: **The process that changes Prime Intellect — Mercy: Prime Intellect permits the living person to act.**

Result: **conditional pass**.

- `mIw` keeps the heading in the domain of a repeatable process, procedure, or formula.
- the changing participant is explicit.
- the subtitle is longer than the English title but remains syntactically transparent.
- `pung` alone is narrower than philosophical grace; the clause must remain attached.

## 2. Philosophical context

Result: **pass for testing**.

The candidate states the chapter's causal and moral structure directly:

1. the process changes the intelligence;
2. the living person remains the actor;
3. the intelligence permits rather than replaces that action.

It does not use physical distance, possession of the person, reciprocal `-chuq`, or an ambiguous purpose clause.

## 3. Literary context

Control: the guide or scaffold may change through encounter, but it must leave the climb to the living climber.

Result: **conditional pass**.

The subtitle preserves the actor/action distinction, but `vang` is intentionally general. It does not itself name climbing, walking, or a physical route and therefore avoids making the literary metaphor mechanically literal.

## Rejection checks

Reject the candidate if review finds that:

- `mIw` reads only as a technical procedure and cannot carry a governing philosophical pattern;
- `pung` makes the subtitle read as pity rather than chosen restraint;
- the sentence-as-object scope does not unambiguously make the living person's action the permitted event;
- the two protected-name occurrences make the title stylistically unacceptable.
