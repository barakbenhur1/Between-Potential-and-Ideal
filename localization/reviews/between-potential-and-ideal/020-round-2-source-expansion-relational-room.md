# Segment 020 source expansion — relational room and protection

This record narrows the next sentence-frame work without manufacturing an inflected target-language phrase. It records lexical directions, grammar limits and explicit non-decisions.

## Source hierarchy

1. Tolkien primary-source locators already recorded in the project remain authoritative for accepted lexical claims.
2. Eldamo is used as a research index and teaching synthesis, not as a replacement for primary-source verification.
3. Any reconstructed, fan-created, course-normalized or period-mixed form remains non-production until its derivation and profile compatibility are documented.
4. No form advances because it merely looks Quenya-like or parses mechanically.

## Relational-room lexical directions

### `nómë`

Recorded teaching-gloss direction: place or location.

Decision: retain as a lexical anchor for testing a transparent “place for another” strategy.

Limits:

- `nómë` alone does not encode relational, moral or epistemic distance;
- it must occur in a construction that identifies who receives or retains the place;
- it must not imply confinement, assigned rank or physical location only;
- the complete phrase still requires primary-source tracing and case review.

### `hye`

Recorded teaching-gloss direction: another person or a distinct third person.

Decision: research direction only.

Limits:

- no directly verified inflected form for the required “for another” role has been recorded in this project;
- do not promote a guessed dative such as `hyen` into a candidate title;
- pronoun paradigms may not be inferred safely from ordinary noun rules without direct evidence;
- the referent must remain a living other person, not an abstract object.

### `quén`

Recorded direction: person or individual.

Decision: retain as a possible explicit-noun alternative to a pronoun-based construction.

Limits:

- a mechanically derived dative or genitive is not yet accepted;
- the construction must still express “another person”, not merely “a person”;
- the stem, case ending and modifier agreement must be verified before a completed phrase is written.

## Case evidence and boundary

The teaching grammar records the dative as a common “to/for” relation and describes regular singular endings for nouns. This is enough to define the grammatical research question, but not enough to approve a specific pronoun or noun phrase for the title.

Therefore:

- `nómë [for another]` remains the approved semantic frame;
- no completed Quenya form is selected;
- neither `Nómë Hyen` nor a derived `nómë quenen` form may be treated as validated;
- the next accepted step requires an attested or fully documented paradigm and a natural-syntax review.

## Protection directions

### `varya-`

Recorded teaching-gloss direction: to protect.

Decision: retain for controlled body-sentence testing, subject to primary-source and period confirmation.

Required sentence distinction:

> the system protects the person from destruction without taking the person's choice

Reject if the completed sentence means that the system guards, owns, confines or commands the person.

### `cauma`

Previously recorded primary-source direction: protection or shelter, PE17/108.

Decision: retain as a sourced nominal anchor, but not as a complete equivalent of agency-preserving protection.

Required qualification:

- what danger is prevented;
- whose agency remains intact;
- how the protection differs from control or enclosure.

`cauma` and `varya-` must not be assumed interchangeable. One is currently tracked as a nominal protection/shelter direction and the other as a verbal research direction; their syntax and semantic scope require separate tests.

## Witness relation direction

### `ara`

Recorded teaching-gloss direction: beside or near.

Decision: retain as a research direction for the witness frame:

> the witness stands beside the person and does not own the experience

Limits:

- the governed case or syntactic construction must be verified;
- physical nearness alone does not encode ethical accompaniment;
- `astarmo` plus a beside-construction still requires the non-possession clause.

## Grace homonym warning

The project retains `lissë` as a grace candidate only under the recorded VT44/18 evidence. Teaching materials may also list a formally identical or related adjective with the sense “sweet”. These entries must not be conflated.

Decision:

- every production use must cite the intended grace source and grammatical category;
- no sentence may rely on a general glossary hit to prove the chapter-specific grace sense;
- chosen relinquishment remains a separate clause even when `lissë` is validly used for grace.

## Powerful-actor warning

A teaching-gloss direction for “powerful” is marked as reconstructed or otherwise non-primary in the course notation.

Decision: do not use it to fill the “powerful actor” slot. The sentence frame must identify the actor transparently using a fully sourced noun phrase or retain the slot unresolved.

## Result for the subtitle strategy

The semantic strategy “Grace as Chosen Release and Room for the Other” remains viable as a split subtitle architecture, but it is not yet expressible as an approved Neo-Quenya string.

What has improved:

- an unsupported abstract distance noun is no longer required;
- “room” can be tested through an explicit place construction;
- the beneficiary can be represented by a person-oriented phrase rather than an invented distance noun;
- protection can be tested separately from control.

What remains blocked:

- the exact inflection for “for another person”;
- a sourced voluntary-yielding clause that does not mean forgiveness, abandonment or defeat;
- natural title syntax and punctuation;
- primary-source and period confirmation for all teaching-gloss directions;
- independent specialist review.
