# Linguistic review — 490 Persons, Plea Bargains, Punishment, and Final Erasure

Both drafts preserve the required legal distinctions: legality and worthiness remain necessary but non-identical; harmed person and accused remain living sources without creating false symmetry between their legal roles; plea bargains remain capable of being responsible translations or coerced appearances of agreement; punishment remains distinct from revenge and pain from proof of justice; the principled opposition to capital punishment remains an argument against final erasure and irreversible state error; society's duty to protect itself remains intact without implying that every person can safely return to free social life. Neither draft is approved.

Klingon blockers: extensive undeclared English remains in the title, headings, and vocabulary of legality, worthiness, living source, harmed person, accused, plea bargain, punishment, proportionality, protection, correction, capital punishment, final erasure, retribution, deterrence, appeal, and irreversible error. Hybrid constructions and English-shaped syntax remain pervasive. One governed Klingon profile is required for distinct legal roles, consent under power imbalance, punishment purposes, correction, permanent removal, protection, and finality.

Neo-Quenya blockers: the title, headings, and most legal terminology remain in ordinary English. English word order and mixed clauses dominate the discussion of legality, worthiness, harmed and accused persons, plea bargains, punishment, capital punishment, protection, appeal, correction, deprivation of liberty, and final erasure. Case marking, compounds, verbal morphology, and the distinctions among legal role, apparent agreement, revenge, protection, correction, irreversible error, and permanent removal require normalization under one Neo-Quenya profile.

Keep both sources as `draft` with `publication: forbidden`.
