# Linguistic review — 460 Rights, Institutions, Force, and Emergency

Both drafts preserve the required distinctions: rights remain institutionally protected human possibilities against power; duties remain reasoned, limited, general, and open to criticism; institutions remain public memory with procedure and accountability; bureaucracy remains necessary but dangerous when procedure replaces the person; corruption remains capture of public mediation rather than only theft; taxation remains coercive while capable of becoming shared capacity through trust and accountability; legal force remains bound by law, oversight, proportionality, necessity, and purpose; emergency remains temporary and explicitly resistant to permanence. Neither draft is approved.

Klingon blockers: extensive undeclared English remains in the title, headings, formulas, and vocabulary of rights, duties, institutions, bureaucracy, corruption, taxation, force, oversight, proportionality, necessity, emergency, public capacity, accountability, appeal, infrastructure, security, and legal procedure. Hybrid constructions and English-shaped syntax remain pervasive. A governed Klingon profile is required for institutional protection, shared obligation, public memory, procedural accountability, corruption, taxation, bounded force, and emergency powers.

Neo-Quenya blockers: the title, headings, formulas, and most legal and institutional terminology remain in ordinary English. English word order and mixed clauses dominate the treatment of rights, duties, institutions, bureaucracy, corruption, taxation, public force, and emergency. Case marking, compounds, verbal morphology, and the distinctions among institutional protection, obligation, procedure, accountability, coercion, legitimacy, and temporary exception require normalization under one Neo-Quenya profile.

Keep both sources as `draft` with `publication: forbidden`.
