# Linguistic review — 390 Engineering, Material, Load, and Foundations

Both drafts preserve the required engineering boundaries: every displayed formula remains a map or bounded technical relation rather than metaphysical proof; blueprint, building, material, load, capacity, and site remain distinct; factor of safety remains technical humility without replacing codes, load factors, limit-state design, or probabilistic reasoning; stress, strain, and Hooke's law are explicitly limited to the simple axial and linear-elastic context; cracks remain information requiring interpretation rather than automatic evidence of danger. Neither draft is approved.

Klingon blockers: extensive undeclared English remains in the title, headings, formulas, and technical vocabulary, including engineering, architecture, material, form, load, capacity, blueprint, building, steel, concrete, compression, tension, reinforcement, prestressing, demand, safety factor, probabilistic reasoning, stress, strain, Hooke's law, crack, foundation, site, and structural information. Hybrid forms and English-shaped syntax remain pervasive. The terminology of loads, materials, capacity, safety, deformation, failure, cracking, foundations, and site conditions requires one governed Klingon profile and full idiomatic recasting.

Neo-Quenya blockers: the title, headings, formulas, and most engineering terminology remain in ordinary English. English word order and mixed clauses dominate descriptions of material behavior, demand and capacity, factor of safety, stress and strain, cracking, and foundations. Case marking, compounds, verbal morphology, technical units, and the distinction among blueprint, built form, material system, load path, capacity, and site require normalization under one Neo-Quenya profile.
