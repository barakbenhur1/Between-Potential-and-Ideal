# Segment 020 opening nonreplacement gate — round 2

Status: semantic-role gate only. No production wording selected. Production source unchanged. Publication forbidden.

This record addresses the source claim that Prime Intellect must not replace the living participant. It does not invent a compact verb for “replace”, and it does not treat simple non-identity as sufficient.

## Source requirement

“Does not replace the living source” requires more than saying that Prime Intellect and the person are different entities. The translation must preserve all of the following:

1. Prime Intellect may speak, formulate, answer, suggest, or illuminate;
2. the living participant remains the actor who travels, chooses, learns, and bears responsibility;
3. Prime Intellect does not perform the participant's morally formative action in the participant's place;
4. the participant is not converted into output, property, representation, or raw material;
5. assistance may remain active without becoming substitution.

## Direct lexical search result

The focused Late-Quenya search did not establish a source-approved verb whose documented sense safely covers replacing a living person or acting in that person's place.

No direct replacement verb is therefore admitted for Neo-Quenya.

The current Klingon review likewise does not admit a direct replacement verb or noun for this clause. A broad negative such as “Prime Intellect does not act” would contradict the source, because Prime Intellect does speak and assist.

## Selected architecture

Nonreplacement is tested through **explicit division of grammatical roles**, followed by a later explicit restraint clause:

- Prime Intellect performs the language or assistance action;
- the living participant performs the lived or formative action;
- every clause names its actor;
- no clause transfers the participant's path, experience, responsibility, or action to Prime Intellect.

This is a semantic architecture, not yet a complete target-language sentence for publication.

# Neo-Quenya (`qya`)

## Controlled role sequence

`Prime Intellect quetë.`

`I coirëa quén lerta lelya tierya.`

Parse:

- `Prime Intellect` — subject of `quetë`, the speech action;
- `i coirëa quén` — subject of `lerta` and the understood infinitive action;
- `lerta lelya` — is free/permitted to travel;
- `tierya` — the living participant's path, documented test formation.

Controlled back-translation:

**Prime Intellect speaks. The living participant remains free to travel their own path.**

## Internal result

The sequence passes the non-substitution role test because:

- Prime Intellect is not the traveler;
- the living participant is not the output object;
- the path is not possessed by Prime Intellect;
- capability and permission remain distinct;
- no animate pronoun classifies Prime Intellect;
- no abstract relation or replacement noun hides agency.

## Neo-Quenya boundary

The sequence does not literally say “Prime Intellect does not replace the living participant”. It establishes the required role structure but leaves an explicit negative replacement or “instead of” construction unresolved.

The following shortcuts are rejected:

- `Prime Intellect lá lelya` — too broad and does not identify whose path is at issue;
- `Prime Intellect lá ná i coirëa quén` — states non-identity, not nonreplacement;
- possession wording in which Prime Intellect has, holds, or owns the person;
- a speculative verb derived solely from an English gloss for replace or substitute;
- returning to `har-`, `lemya-`, `ser-`, `mar-`, `harya-`, or `ovantë`.

Neo-Quenya gate:

- grammatical role separation complete: **yes**;
- participant retains path/action: **yes**;
- direct replacement lexeme admitted: **no**;
- explicit negative nonreplacement clause complete: **no**;
- production use allowed: **no**.

# Klingon (`tlh`)

## Controlled role sequence

`jatlh Prime Intellect.`

`vang yInbogh ghot.`

`vang yInbogh ghot 'e' chaw' Prime Intellect.`

Parse:

- `jatlh Prime Intellect` — Prime Intellect speaks;
- `vang yInbogh ghot` — the living person acts;
- the sentence-as-object construction makes the person's action the event Prime Intellect permits;
- Prime Intellect does not become the grammatical subject of the person's action.

Controlled back-translation:

**Prime Intellect speaks. The living person acts. Prime Intellect permits the living person's action.**

## Internal result

The sequence passes the non-substitution role test because:

- the person remains subject of `vang`;
- Prime Intellect permits the event rather than performing it;
- the person is not the direct object processed or controlled;
- no physical `He` or `chuq` carries the ethical meaning;
- no generic “worthy” predicate substitutes for the conduct.

## Klingon boundary

The sequence does not literally contain “replace” or “instead of”. It establishes actor separation but leaves a direct negative replacement clause unresolved.

The following shortcuts are rejected:

- `vangbe' Prime Intellect` — falsely claims Prime Intellect does not act at all;
- a control clause presented as benign replacement;
- a physical path term used as the sole proof of agency;
- an unverified replacement lexeme or English stem with Klingon morphology;
- sentence compression that makes the subject of `vang` ambiguous.

Klingon gate:

- grammatical role separation complete: **yes**;
- participant remains actor: **yes**;
- permission architecture complete for tests: **yes**;
- direct replacement lexeme admitted: **no**;
- explicit negative nonreplacement clause complete: **no**;
- production use allowed: **no**.
