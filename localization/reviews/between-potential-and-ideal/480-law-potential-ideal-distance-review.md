# Linguistic review — 480 Law, Justice, Evidence, and Legal Distance

Both drafts preserve the required legal distinctions: law remains moral translation under limitation rather than a replacement for truth or justice; the lens remains broader than criminal law while retaining criminal-law examples; legal potential remains the possibility of not adding injustice to injustice; the institutional-justice relation remains a warning map rather than a legal formula; legal ideal, procedural justice, and local legal optimum remain distinct; legal distance remains both an unavoidable gap and an intentionally created safeguard. Neither draft is approved.

Klingon blockers: extensive undeclared English remains in the title, headings, relation map, and vocabulary of law, justice, evidence, procedure, proportionality, access, bias, automation, criminal, civil, administrative, constitutional, contractual, family, compensation, acquittal, legal ideal, procedural justice, horizon, presumption of innocence, representation, and appeal. Additional mixed-language contamination appears in the prose. Hybrid constructions and English-shaped syntax remain pervasive. One governed Klingon profile is required for legal fields, evidence, procedure, proportionality, correction, admissibility, moral versus legal guilt, horizon, and institutional safeguards.

Neo-Quenya blockers: the title, headings, relation map, and nearly all legal terminology remain in ordinary English. English word order and mixed clauses dominate the discussion of legal fields, evidence, procedure, compensation, punishment, acquittal, proportionality, legal ideal, local optimum, horizon, presumption of innocence, representation, and appeal. Case marking, compounds, verbal morphology, and the distinctions among truth, proof, admissibility, moral guilt, legal guilt, distance, correction, and institutional safeguard require normalization under one Neo-Quenya profile.
