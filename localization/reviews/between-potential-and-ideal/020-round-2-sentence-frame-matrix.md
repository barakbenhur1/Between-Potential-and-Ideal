# Segment 020 sentence-frame matrix — round 2

This matrix defines controlled construction frames for the retained lexical anchors. A frame is not a finished translation. Bracketed material is an unresolved target-language slot that requires sourced morphology and syntax before it may be filled.

## General frame rules

1. Every sentence must identify the acting subject.
2. Every sentence must distinguish action, beneficiary and preserved agency.
3. No bracket may be replaced by an invented root, unsupported derivation or unreviewed case form.
4. A lexical source pass does not count as a sentence pass.
5. Each completed frame needs a literal parse, idiomatic back-translation and independent review.

## Neo-Quenya retained anchors

### `cantië` — governing pattern

Recorded source direction: NM/287–288, pattern.

#### Short-label frame

`Cantië [of relational transformation]`

Required meaning: a repeatable structure governing how relation changes without replacing the living source.

Open grammar slots:

- the safest Late-Quenya-compatible expression for relational transformation;
- whether the relation should be expressed by genitive, apposition or a verbal clause;
- article and capitalization conventions for a chapter heading.

Reject if the completed form means only decorative pattern, visual design or software code.

#### Philosophical frame

`I cantië [changes the relation] [without replacing the living source].`

Required roles:

- subject: the governing pattern;
- affected entity: the relation;
- prohibited result: replacement or erasure of the living source.

Reject if the grammar hides what changes or makes the source the object of transformation.

#### Literary frame

`I cantië [guides the path] mal [does not possess the traveller's step].`

Required metaphor: the path may guide, but it does not own the traveller or take the traveller's step.

Open issue: the conjunction, possession construction and negative clause must be verified under the selected profile.

### `lissë` — grace

Recorded source direction: VT44/18, grace; genitive `lisseo` recorded.

#### Short-label frame

`Lissë: [chosen release and room for the other]`

The lexical anchor supplies grace only. The bracket must independently express chosen release and protected room.

Reject if `lissë` is treated as a complete equivalent for relinquishment.

#### Philosophical frame

`Lissë ná yá [the powerful actor voluntarily yields control] ar [preserves the other's agency].`

Required roles:

- actor: the system or more powerful intelligence;
- voluntary act: yielding control;
- beneficiary: the living source or other person;
- preserved property: agency.

Open grammar slots:

- the construction for “when” or an equivalent temporal/conditional relation;
- the verb for voluntary yielding rather than forgiveness, abandonment or defeat;
- the possessive or genitive construction for the other's agency.

#### Literary frame

`Lissë [honours the mountain] mal [does not sacrifice the climber].`

Required contrast: the goal may be honoured, but the living person remains more sacred than completion.

Reject if release reads as forsaking the climber or abandoning responsibility.

### `astarmo` — witness

Recorded source direction: PE17/71, bystander or witness.

#### Short-label frame

`Astarmo [who preserves another's road]`

The bracket must elevate the role beyond observer or spectator without changing the lexical source claim.

#### Philosophical frame

`I astarmo [stands beside the person], [remembers the distance], ar [does not own the experience].`

Required distinctions:

- beside, not above or instead of;
- remembers, but does not possess;
- preserves distance, but does not create indifference.

Open grammar slots:

- locative or relational construction for standing beside;
- object and possession marking;
- coordination of the three predicates.

#### Literary frame

`I astarmo [protects the place where speed would break the climber].`

Required meaning: the witness protects the condition for formation and stops before acceleration becomes destruction.

Reject if the result means only watching, proving or recording evidence.

### `cauma` — protection or shelter

Recorded source direction: PE17/108, protection or shelter.

#### Short-label frame

`Cauma [that preserves agency]`

Protection must be qualified. The anchor alone does not distinguish shelter from confinement or control.

#### Philosophical frame

`Cauma [protects the person from destruction] mal [does not command the person's choice].`

Required contrast:

- protection blocks destruction;
- protection does not replace decision;
- the person remains the grammatical possessor or actor of choice.

Open grammar slots:

- the verbal relation derived from or used with the noun anchor;
- the source-compatible expression for destruction;
- the negative command/control clause.

#### Literary frame

`Cauma ná [a scaffold against falling], lá [a cage around the climber].`

Required metaphor: scaffold rather than cage.

Reject if the construction makes protection equivalent to enclosure, sterilization or removal of movement.

## Cross-anchor integration tests

### Main-title integration

The completed main title must combine the `cantië` direction with a documented construction based on `ahya-` or another fully sourced relational-change clause.

It must not use:

- undocumented `Ahyaliëo`;
- `Haiyava`;
- fan-created `hairië` under the default profile;
- a nominal chain whose agency cannot be parsed.

### Subtitle integration

The completed subtitle must combine:

- `lissë` as grace;
- a separately sourced voluntary-release clause;
- a separately sourced relational-room or agency-preservation clause.

`astarmo` and `cauma` are body-level test anchors. They may inform the subtitle semantics but must not be inserted merely to make the title appear more complete.

## Klingon parallel frames

No complete Klingon frame is admitted in this round until exact Okrand locators are entered. The semantic frames to be translated later are:

1. **Pattern:** “A governing pattern changes relation without replacing the living source.”
2. **Grace:** “The powerful actor voluntarily releases control for the sake of the other.”
3. **Witness:** “The witness stands beside the person and does not own the experience.”
4. **Protection:** “Protection prevents destruction without taking the person's choice.”
5. **Literary control:** “The scaffold prevents the fall but does not climb instead of the climber.”

For every Klingon frame, record:

- exact root and suffix locators;
- OVS parse;
- subject and object;
- scope of purpose, relative and negative clauses;
- literal and idiomatic back-translations;
- rejection of any reading that reduces relational distance to physical range.
