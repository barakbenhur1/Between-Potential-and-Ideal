# Linguistic review — 550 Boundary Horizons

Both drafts preserve the required distinctions: a horizon remains a relation among observer, tool, condition, and field of possibilities rather than merely an edge or wall; inaccessibility remains distinct from nonexistence across education, law, medicine, physics, AI, art, and governance; double humility remains exact—do not erase and do not fantasize; the action-under-a-horizon relation remains a responsibility map rather than an empirical formula; physical event horizons and cosmic event horizons remain scientifically bounded and distinct from metaphorical uses; domain-specific horizons of evidence, understanding, diagnosis, and explanation remain present; the ending requires honesty about what is not known rather than total knowledge. Neither draft is approved.

Klingon blockers: extensive undeclared English remains in the title, headings, relation map, and vocabulary of horizon, observer, tool, condition, field, accessibility, uncertainty, correction, projection, authority, event horizon, cosmic event horizon, evidence, diagnosis, explanation, AI, black box, and responsibility. Hybrid constructions and English-shaped syntax remain pervasive. A governed Klingon profile is required for observer-relative access, uncertainty marking, correction, projection, physical versus metaphorical horizons, and epistemic honesty.

Neo-Quenya blockers: the title, headings, relation map, and most epistemic and scientific terminology remain in ordinary English. English word order and mixed clauses dominate the treatment of observer, instrument, accessibility, nonexistence, uncertainty, projection, physical horizons, evidence, understanding, diagnosis, explanation, and authority. Case marking, compounds, verbal morphology, and the distinctions among inaccessible, nonexistent, unknown, unmeasured, inferable, and correctable require normalization under one Neo-Quenya profile.
