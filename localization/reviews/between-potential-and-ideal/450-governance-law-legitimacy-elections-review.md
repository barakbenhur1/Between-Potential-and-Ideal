# Linguistic review — 450 Governance, Law, Legitimacy, and Elections

Both drafts preserve the required political distinctions: governance remains mediation of plural human potential rather than proof of social harmony; the state of nature remains recurring collapse of trusted mediation rather than only ancient history; law remains both limit and possible condition of shared freedom; legitimacy remains distinct from coercive compliance and authority from truth; separation of powers remains mutual limitation in service of public trust; elections remain legitimate aggregation of preference rather than measurement of philosophical truth. Neither draft is approved.

Klingon blockers: extensive undeclared English remains in the title, headings, formulas, and vocabulary of governance, government, law, courts, police, military, elections, procedures, regulations, state of nature, mediation, legitimacy, sovereignty, authority, separation of powers, democracy, accountability, coercion, corruption, and public trust. Additional mixed-language contamination appears in the text. Hybrid constructions and English-shaped syntax remain pervasive. One governed Klingon profile is required for institutions, lawfulness, legitimacy, sovereignty, authority, elections, democracy, public trust, and procedural fairness.

Neo-Quenya blockers: the title, headings, formulas, and nearly all political and legal terminology remain in ordinary English. English word order and mixed clauses dominate the treatment of governance, state of nature, law, legitimacy, sovereignty, separation of powers, elections, and democracy. Case marking, compounds, verbal morphology, institutional vocabulary, and the distinctions among force, authority, legitimacy, truth, preference, and public accountability require normalization under one Neo-Quenya profile.

Keep both sources as `draft` with `publication: forbidden`.
