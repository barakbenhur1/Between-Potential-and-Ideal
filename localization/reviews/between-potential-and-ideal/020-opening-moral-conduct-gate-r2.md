# Neo-Quenya (`qya`)

## Source direction

The pinned Late-Quenya grammar records:

- `lerta-` — be free to do, be permitted to do;
- modal use at VT41/5–6;
- an example pattern `lertan menë ...`, “I may/am free to go ...”.

This verb describes the acting participant's freedom. It does not require an unverified causative claim that Prime Intellect creates or grants that freedom.

## Controlled test

`I coirëa quén lerta lelya tierya.`

Parse:

- `i coirëa quén` — subject, “the living person”;
- `lerta` — singular aorist of `lerta-`, “is free/permitted”;
- `lelya` — infinitive complement, “to travel/proceed”;
- `tie-rya` > `tierya` — the participant's path, documented test formation.

Literal back-translation:

**The living person is free to travel his or her path.**

Controlled contextual back-translation:

**The living participant remains free to travel their own path.**

## Decision

The clause passes the internal agency-preservation test because the living participant is:

- grammatical subject;
- bearer of freedom;
- traveler;
- possessor of the path.

The clause does not claim:

- that Prime Intellect owns or grants the freedom;
- that Prime Intellect is animate;
- that Prime Intellect is morally worthy by adjective;
- that the path is physical only;
- that `lerta-` means protection, grace, or non-replacement.

Neo-Quenya limits:

- `tierya` still requires external morphology review;
- the idiomatic combination `lerta lelya` requires external review;
- explicit Prime Intellect restraint is not yet stated;
- explicit non-replacement is not yet stated.

# Klingon (`tlh`)

## Retained controlled test

`vang yInbogh ghot 'e' chaw' Prime Intellect.`

Parse:

- `vang` — acts/takes action;
- `yIn-bogh ghot` — “the person who lives”, subject of `vang`;
- `'e'` — the preceding sentence as object;
- `chaw'` — allows or permits;
- `Prime Intellect` — subject of `chaw'`.

Literal back-translation:

**Prime Intellect permits that the living person acts.**

Idiomatic back-translation:

**Prime Intellect allows the living person to act.**

## Decision

The clause passes the internal agency-preservation test because:

- the living person is the actor of `vang`;
- Prime Intellect permits the event rather than performing it;
- the permitted event is the complete action clause, not the person as an extracted object;
- no physical path or distance noun is required;
- capability, usefulness, and moral worth remain separate.

Klingon limits:

- external review of sentence-as-object scope and title/body style remains required;
- the clause does not yet say Prime Intellect stops;
- the clause does not yet explicitly deny replacement or control;
- `pung` is not part of this test and receives no additional approval here.

## Why the structures need not be literal mirrors

Neo-Quenya safely states the participant's freedom without inventing a causative “make free/allow” construction. Klingon safely states permission through its sentence-as-object construction. The structures differ, but the living participant remains the actor in both.

This is acceptable for internal semantic parity. It is not external linguistic approval.

## Rejection conditions

Reject any later integration if:

- Prime Intellect becomes the subject of `lelya`, `vang`, learning, choosing, or lived experience in place of the participant;
- `lerta-` is expanded to mean grace, protection, or moral worth without separate evidence;
- the Klingon sentence-as-object scope is altered so the person becomes the processed object;
- either language introduces a positive adjective as a substitute for conduct;
- the clause implies that permission is enough even when the system later takes control.
