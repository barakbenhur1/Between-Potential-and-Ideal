# Segment 020 clause test — round 2

Status: test only. Publication forbidden. Production source unchanged.

## A. Directed speech to the person

`Prime Intellect quetë i coirëa quenen.`

Parse:
- `Prime Intellect`: subject, protected name.
- `quetë`: singular aorist of `quet-`.
- `i coirëa quenen`: dative recipient, “to the living person”.

Literal back-translation: Prime Intellect speaks to the living person.

Internal second-pass result: pass for controlled tests. External specialist confirmation remains required.

## B. Directed speech back to the intelligence

Review notation:

`I coirëa quén quetë Prime Intellect-enna.`

Parse:
- `i coirëa quén`: subject, “the living person”.
- `quetë`: singular aorist of `quet-`.
- `Prime Intellect-enna`: protected proper name plus visible allative review suffix, “to Prime Intellect”.

Primary construction control: PM/401 records a `quet-` speech clause with the proper-name allative `Elendilenna`.

Literal back-translation: The living person speaks to Prime Intellect.

Internal second-pass result: preferred test route. The hyphen is review notation; final suffix styling on the two-word English name is not approved.

Rejected alternative:

`I coirëa quén quetë sena.`

Reason: `sena` is an attested long dative of animate `se`, but applying it to Prime Intellect would silently impose an animate classification that the segment does not establish.

## C. Agency and path

`I coirëa quén lelya tierya.`

Parse:
- `i coirëa quén`: subject, “the living person”.
- `lelya`: singular aorist of `lelya-`, “travels/proceeds”.
- `tie-rya`: path plus third-person singular possessive, written as `tierya`.

Literal back-translation: The living person travels his/her path.

Contextual back-translation: The living participant travels their own path.

Internal second-pass result:
- `lelya`: pass for controlled tests; the pinned grammar explicitly gives `i atan lelya` “the man travels”.
- `tierya`: retained as a documented test formation. Attested `tielyanna` at UT/22 establishes the suffix-bearing stem `tie-`; sourced `-rya` supplies the third-person possessive.
- contextual “own”: acceptable only as an idiomatic back-translation; the literal parse remains “his/her path”.
