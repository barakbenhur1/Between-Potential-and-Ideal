Glossary

A compact guide to the recurring terms of Between Potential and Ideal. The definitions are not slogans; they are reading tools for moving through the essays, stories, and applications.

On this pagePotentialIdealOptimalSourceWitnessDistance and frictionMetaphor and proofRelated reading

Potential

Potential is the field of what may become present before it has taken a clarified form. It includes ability, pressure, possibility, unfinished direction, and the surplus from which many different outcomes may appear.

Potential is not automatically good. A possibility can become generous, destructive, shallow, beautiful, useful, or false. The ethical question begins when potential receives shape.

Ideal

Ideal means morally clarified possibility: not simply what can happen, but what would be worthy of becoming real. It is a direction of value, not a decorative fantasy of perfection.

The ideal protects the project from treating every successful realization as justified merely because it succeeded. It asks what a thing ought to become, not only what it can become.

Optimal

Optimal is the local translation of the ideal under real constraints. It is what can responsibly be done inside time, body, budget, law, fear, uncertainty, fatigue, and incomplete knowledge.

The optimal is not a surrender of the ideal. It is the attempt to carry the ideal without pretending that the world has removed all friction.

Source

The source is the living origin before extraction: the person, experience, scene, wound, question, or reality from which a statement is taken. The project repeatedly warns against honoring an idea while forgetting the living source that made it possible.

Witness

A witness does not own the truth. It stands near it, records it, tests it, and remains answerable to what exceeds it. In this project, AI may be a mirror, witness, or tool, but not the owner of lived experience.

Distance and friction

Distance is the space between answer and understanding. Friction is the resistance through which knowledge becomes lived rather than merely obtained. A shortcut may produce a result while still weakening understanding.

Metaphor and proof

Scientific, mathematical, and computational terms are used carefully. When the site makes a formal claim, it must be treated as a claim. When it uses a term as a structural metaphor, the metaphor should clarify rather than pretend to prove.
